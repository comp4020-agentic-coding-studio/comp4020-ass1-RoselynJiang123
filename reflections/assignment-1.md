# Assignment 1 Reflection

The breakthrough that moved the project forward was realising that an educational website can be technically correct and detailed, yet still fail if people cannot understand how to use it. After I completed Stages 1–3, I asked a friend to try the page without guidance. She said, "It looks professional, but I do not know what you are trying to tell me." Because I discovered this only halfway through the project, I had to redesign much of the page and spend extra time.

This also taught me that "the tests pass" and "the explanation works" are different judgements. Automated checks could protect scientific direction, state transitions, keyboard behaviour, and rules such as a Stage 2 drag not changing frequency. They could not tell me that the first unfold animation looked like an image being flattened and removed, or that a large hotspot covered the anatomy it was meant to reveal. I began treating these observations as evidence, not just personal taste. Instead of asking the agent to make the page "more polished," I gave it observable constraints, rejected the failed transition, and turned reusable lessons into CLAUDE.md rules or regression tests.

This assignment also changed the kind of developer I want to become. Because of my nursing background, I first thought responsibility mainly meant checking medical facts. I now understand that it also means controlling what an interaction suggests. Audio should not be required to continue; approximate sound labels should not look like strict biological boundaries; and animation should not control application state. I want to use agents for speed without giving away my judgement. I want to define the idea clearly, decide what evidence is needed to trust it, and discard an attractive solution when real users understand the wrong message.
